Write a goal. Loom drives it to verified.

Loom is a software factory for AI coding subscriptions. It turns a goal into tickets, builds them in parallel, reviews and fixes the work, then verifies it against the goal.

The factory

Small steps carry the work.

Labels mark each ticket's state so the brain, station and lanes can coordinate.

queued→briefed→running→pr→merged→shipped→verified
  1. 01

    Goal

    Describe what done means, how progress is measured, and the rules for shipping.

  2. 02

    Brain

    Measures progress, ranks open problems, and writes tickets for useful next steps.

  3. 03

    Ticket

    States why the work matters, what to change, and how to prove it works.

  4. 04

    Station and lanes

    The station gives each ticket to a free lane. Every lane works in its own clone and branch.

  5. 05

    Review

    Automated review and CI check each pull request. Findings go back to the lane that wrote it.

  6. 06

    Merge, ship, verify

    A ticket is done when its proof is available and the goal's measure moves.

  7. 07

    Journal and report

    Each cycle records what happened and asks for help only when a person needs to act.

It improves itself

Loom can work on Loom.

When a recurring slowdown is worth fixing, it becomes a ticket. The improvement counts when the chosen measure changes.

noticedA repeated review finding points to a process gap.
ticketedA small change is described with checks.
shippedThe change passes review and reaches its target.
movedThe measure is checked again to confirm progress.
Lanes by difficulty

Match the work to a lane.

localsubscription CLIhosted provider
easyUse a local or low-cost lane when the change is clear and contained.
mediumUse a general coding lane for work that needs a few decisions.
hardRoute difficult or stuck work to a stronger lane under your own configuration.
The rules
One ticket, one branch, one pull request. Lanes do not merge.
Checks should fail without the fix and pass with it.
Merged is not shipped, and shipped is not verified.
Each operator supplies and controls their own credentials.
